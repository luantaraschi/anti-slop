# Presence

Is there a person here?

Six tells on what the text says about who wrote it. Five of them fire on
something present that should not be, and they are safe. The sixth, `P5`, fires
on something absent, which makes it the one tell in this catalog that can push a
rewrite into adding a position the author never held. Read its exemptions before
you fire it, and when the register is in doubt, do not.

### P1 — Chatbot residue left in the text

**Signal**  Lines addressed to whoever commissioned the text, pasted into it: *I hope this helps*, *let me know if you would like me to expand on any section*, *here is an overview of*, *would you like me to*, *feel free to ask*. In Portuguese, *espero ter ajudado*, *fico à disposição*, *segue abaixo*, *qualquer dúvida, estou à disposição*.

**Principle**  Correspondence is not content. The reader of the finished text was never party to the exchange it came out of.

**Fix**  Cut the line. Nothing replaces it.

**Not slop when**  the document genuinely is correspondence, such as an email or a support reply, and the line is addressed to its actual recipient.

### P2 — Sycophancy

**Signal**  Praise directed at the reader or at the question: *great question*, *you are absolutely right*, *an excellent point*, *that is a really interesting way to look at it*, *certainly*, *of course*.

**Principle**  Agreement offered before the argument is not agreement, it is a lubricant. It costs the writer credibility on everything that follows.

**Fix**  Cut the praise and keep the substance. Where the text really is responding to someone, respond to what they said rather than to the fact that they said it.

**Not slop when**  the register is genuinely a reply to a person and the acknowledgement is specific rather than formulaic.

### P3 — The fake candid opener

**Signal**  A theatrical pause before an ordinary claim: *Honestly?*, *Look,*, *Here is the thing*, *The thing is*, *Let us be honest*, *Real talk*. In Portuguese, *Olha,*, *Sinceramente,*, *Vamos ser francos*, *A real é que*. The shape is a one word question or aside, then the supposed truth.

**Principle**  The opener manufactures intimacy to make a routine claim feel earned. A person being candid usually just says the thing.

**Fix**  Delete the opener and start at the claim. The claim is almost always unchanged by the deletion, which is the evidence that the opener was doing nothing.

**Not slop when**  the word appears mid sentence in ordinary casual writing, which is not this tell, or the author's sample opens paragraphs this way.

### P4 — Coaching the reader

**Signal**  Second person instruction where none was asked for: *you need to understand that*, *remember, X is important*, *think of it like*, *the key takeaway here is*, *ask yourself whether*.

**Principle**  It casts the reader as a student and the writer as an instructor, in a document that is neither a lesson nor addressed to anyone in particular. It also inflates length, because every claim arrives twice, once as instruction and once as content.

**Fix**  State the thing. A reader who needs the analogy will build one; a reader who does not has been slowed down.

**Not slop when**  the document is teaching material, a tutorial, or a guide whose whole contract is instruction.

### P5 — Neutrality where the genre wants a position

**Signal**  A text whose genre is opinion, review, recommendation or argument, and which reaches the end without holding one: every option presented with balanced advantages, every judgment deferred, a conclusion that says it depends and stops.

**Principle**  Bloodless neutrality is as clear a sign of an unread draft as inflation is. A recommendation that recommends nothing has not been written, it has been surveyed.

**Fix**  Say what the writer thinks, using only what the source already contains. Where the source contains no judgment, say that the text does not reach one and why, which is itself a position.

**Not slop when**  the register requires neutrality, and it usually does: encyclopedic, technical, legal, procedural, reference, news reporting, minutes, specifications. In all of those, plain and neutral is the human voice and adding stance is the defect. Fire this tell only where the genre is unmistakably one that takes a position, and never invent the position: a stance the source does not support is a fabrication under the rule that governs those.

### P6 — Prose written against a diff

**Signal**  Documentation or comments narrating a change rather than describing the thing: *this replaces the previous approach*, *we have now added*, *unlike before*, *this was updated to*, *no longer uses*.

**Principle**  The reader arrives at the document without the previous version. Prose that only makes sense next to what it replaced ages into prose that makes no sense at all.

**Fix**  Describe what the thing is and does now. Where the history matters, it belongs in a changelog, which is the document whose job that is.

**Not slop when**  the document is version scoped by definition: a changelog, a release note, a migration guide, a pull request description, a commit message.
