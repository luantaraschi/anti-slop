# Hollow

Is there anything behind this sentence?

Nine tells on the sentence that occupies the place information should hold. The
axis is about weight, not about tone: a sentence can be perfectly sober and
still carry nothing. Run every one of them through the rule in four places, and
give particular attention to the second, because a sentence carrying one hard
fact is not hollow no matter how it is dressed.

### H1 — Significance nobody measured

**Signal**  A clause asserting what something represents rather than what it did: *marked a pivotal moment*, *a testament to*, *underscores the importance of*, *reflects a broader shift*, *played a crucial role in*, *set the stage for*, *left an indelible mark*.

**Principle**  Importance is shown by what happened. Claimed alongside the fact, it is the writer telling the reader how to feel about a thing the writer has not made interesting.

**Fix**  Keep the event, cut the claim about its meaning. Where the significance is real and known, state the consequence instead: what changed, for whom, when.

**Not slop when**  the text carries a source that measured it, or the document's whole job is to argue significance, as in a review, an obituary or a citation.

### H2 — Notability counted by volume

**Signal**  Notability established by list length: outlets a person has appeared in, follower counts, awards enumerated without occasion, *has been featured in*, *widely recognised as*.

**Principle**  A list of places a claim appeared is not the claim. Ten unexplained citations tell the reader less than one with its context.

**Fix**  Keep the one with context and say what was said there. Cut the rest. Never invent the context to make the trimmed version land.

**Not slop when**  the document is a press kit, a bibliography or a CV, where the enumeration is the deliverable.

### H3 — A promotional adjective with no fact behind it

**Signal**  Capability, quality or scale asserted by adjective: *powerful*, *seamless*, *robust*, *vibrant*, *cutting edge*, *world class*, *breathtaking*, *stunning*, *nestled in the heart of*. The intensifier is the compressed form of the same failure.

**Principle**  The reader wants to know what the thing does. An adjective claiming it is good occupies the space where that would go, and costs the writer credit besides.

**Fix**  Replace with what the thing does, at the size the source supports. If the source supports nothing, cut the sentence rather than soften the adjective.

**Not slop when**  the brand voice is openly promotional and the claim is backed in the same breath by something checkable.

### H4 — An authority with no name

**Signal**  An opinion attributed to an unnamed body: *experts argue*, *industry reports suggest*, *observers have noted*, *studies show*, *it is widely believed*, *many argue*.

**Principle**  An unnamed authority is the writer's own opinion wearing a borrowed coat. The reader cannot check it and cannot weigh it.

**Fix**  Name the source, or state the claim as the writer's and let it stand on its argument. Never invent a source to make a sentence look sourced.

**Not slop when**  the attribution is anonymous for a reason the text states, such as a source who cannot be named, or the body of work is genuinely too large to cite in one sentence and the text cites it properly elsewhere.

### H5 — A guess wearing the clothes of a fact

**Signal**  Speculation written in the register of record: *likely began*, *it is believed that*, *would have been*, *presumably*, *appears to have*, followed by a detail no source supports. In biography it lands on the same stock guesses: *maintains a low profile*, *prefers to stay out of the spotlight*, *keeps personal details private*.

**Principle**  The reader cannot tell a hedged guess from a hedged fact, so the hedge does not protect them. What the writer did not find is not evidence about the subject.

**Fix**  Say what is not known, or cut the sentence. Never fill a gap with what would plausibly go there.

**Not slop when**  the text is explicitly reasoning about an unknown and shows its reasoning, so the reader can weigh the inference.

### H6 — A conclusion that would end any text

**Signal**  A closing paragraph that would fit unchanged on a different subject: *the future looks bright*, *exciting times ahead*, *a step in the right direction*, *only time will tell*, *one thing is certain*.

**Principle**  A send-off is what a text writes when it has run out of things to say and has not noticed. It teaches the reader that the last paragraph is safe to skip.

**Fix**  Cut it. End on the last concrete thing the text knows. Where the source states real plans, use those instead.

**Not slop when**  the genre requires a closing move, as in a speech, a letter or a formal report with a stated conclusion.

### H7 — The aphorism formula

**Signal**  An ordinary claim rebuilt as a reusable maxim: *X is the Y of Z*, *X is not a tool but a mirror*, *the language of trust*, *the currency of attention*, *the architecture of*, *X becomes a trap when*.

**Principle**  The formula sounds like precision and is the opposite: it trades a claim the reader could disagree with for one they cannot get hold of.

**Fix**  Write the concrete claim the formula is gesturing at, including whatever qualification made it too dull to say straight.

**Not slop when**  the line is quoted from someone, or the text is aphoristic throughout by the author's evident habit.

### H8 — The deeper truth trope

**Signal**  A phrase announcing that ceremony is about to be cut through: *the real question is*, *at its core*, *what really matters is*, *fundamentally*, *the deeper issue here*, *the heart of the matter*, *let us be clear*.

**Principle**  The sentence that follows almost always restates an ordinary point with extra ceremony. The announcement is the ceremony.

**Fix**  Delete the frame and keep the claim. If the claim cannot stand without the frame, the frame was carrying it and the claim was not worth the sentence.

**Not slop when**  the text has genuinely just cleared away a stated confusion and is naming what survived it.

### H9 — A paragraph about what the writer could not find

**Signal**  Prose narrating the writer's own search: *while specific details are limited*, *based on available information*, *as of my last update*, *not extensively documented in readily available sources*, *further research would be needed*.

**Principle**  This is a note to the person who commissioned the text, left in the text. The reader did not ask what the writer's afternoon was like.

**Fix**  State the gap in one plain clause if the gap matters to the reader, and cut the paragraph if it does not.

**Not slop when**  the document is a research note, a literature review or a status report, where the state of the evidence is the subject.
