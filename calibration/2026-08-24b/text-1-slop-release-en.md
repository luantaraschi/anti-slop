# text-1-slop-release-en

## What I judged the document to be, and who reads it

A product release announcement (a changelog / "what's new" post) for Tideline,
a tidal-planning app, version 2.3. The stated audience is professional guides —
people who plan trips around tides for a living. Register: public,
version-scoped release note, part promotional, part factual changelog. No
author voice sample was supplied (root 1 unanswered — see below); the rewrite
falls back to plain, as the skill directs when that root goes unanswered.

**Root left unanswered:** root 1, the voice. No sample of the author's own
writing was given, and none is recoverable from the source text itself
(the source is uniformly inflated, so it gives no baseline to match). The
rewrite uses the stated fallback — plain — rather than inventing a house
voice. Nobody was available to ask, per the task instructions, so this is
flagged here rather than left silent.

## The note I would have delivered

This release note buried three real facts — offline charts, a rebuilt
prediction engine, and dropped iOS 15 support — under sign-off boilerplate,
an empty "What's Next" section, and a stray line of chatbot correspondence at
the end. The rewrite keeps the facts, cuts the send-off material, and states
the hedged forecasts once each instead of twice.

## Question 1 — rules I had to supply that a tell does not contain

- **T1, "three of everything."** The tell's own words: *"the tell fires when
  three is the most common count and no section departs from it."* That
  wording assumes a text long enough to have a "most common count" across
  several sections. This document has exactly two enumerable structures — the
  "accuracy, reliability, and confidence" triple and the three-item feature
  list — both threes, in a 340-word text. The tell gives no minimum sample
  size, so I supplied one: two triples, adjacent, in a document this short,
  count as the pattern the tell is naming. A longer document with the same
  two triples buried in it might not have fired.

- **G3, "synonym cycling."** Signal: *"the protagonist, then the main
  character, then the central figure, then the hero."* The source calls the
  product "the most powerful tidal planning platform," then "Tideline"
  repeatedly, then "the definitive tool for tidal planning" at the very end.
  That is two competing generic labels bookending a short text, not the
  three-or-more-name churn the example shows. The tell does not say how many
  distinct labels or how close together they must sit to count. I supplied
  the threshold — at least three competing near-adjacent labels — and on
  that basis declined to fire it. A stricter reading could have fired it.

- **G10, "the uniformly hyphenated compound."** Signal requires seeing the
  same compound *"hyphenated everywhere regardless of position"* — which
  needs at least two positions (attributive and predicative) to compare.
  The source has exactly one hyphenated compound, "cutting-edge," used once.
  The tell never states a minimum instance count, so I supplied one: a single
  occurrence cannot establish a pattern of uniformity across positions, so it
  cannot fire on principle, not just in this instance.

- **H5 vs. G5, "a guess wearing the clothes of a fact" vs. "hedging in
  layers."** *"we believe this will likely become the standard workflow"*
  sits on the boundary between the two. H5's signal is speculation *"written
  in the register of record"* with no source; but this sentence is
  explicitly attributed ("we believe"), which is closer to G5's *"could
  potentially... may possibly"* stacking than to H5's undressed guess. The
  catalog does not say which tell wins when a sentence is both hedged and
  attributed. I supplied the rule that explicit self-attribution moves a
  speculative sentence out of H5 and into G5, and filed it there instead of
  double-counting it.

## Question 2 — for every tell that did not fire, which of three ways did it decline

**Condition never arose (18):**
H2 (no notability-by-volume list), H4 (the one attributed opinion is
self-sourced, not an anonymous authority), H7 (no "X is the Y of Z" mirror
formula distinct from the T2 instance already counted), H9 (no
search-disclaimer language), T3 (no "from X to Y" range), T6 (no
concede-then-recover difficulties section), T8 (no run of three-plus short
staccato fragments), G7 (no nominalisation), G8 (no run of paragraph-opening
connectives), M6 (no decorative bullets or arrows), P2 (no praise directed at
a reader), P3 (no fake-candid opener).

**Condition arose, a "Not slop when" clause excused it (2):**
T9 ("one measure for every paragraph") — the document is short enough that
paragraph-length spread cannot be measured, which is T9's own exemption.
P6 ("prose written against a diff") — the iOS 15 paragraph does use
diff-shaped language ("no longer"), but the whole document is a release note,
which P6 exempts by name as a version-scoped genre.

**Condition arose, but the judgment call under Q1 excused it (2):**
G3 (synonym cycling) — two labels, not the three-plus churn the tell
describes; declined under the threshold I supplied above, not because the
text had already handled it well.
H5 (guess dressed as fact) — the sentence is genuinely hedged and genuinely
speculative, but the explicit "we believe" attribution is what moved it to
G5 instead of firing here, per the rule I supplied above.

**Condition arose too thinly to establish the pattern (1):**
G10 (uniformly hyphenated compound) — one instance, not enough positions to
compare, per the threshold I supplied above.

That accounts for all 18 tells that did not fire (9 Hollow − 4 fired = 5
declined; 9 Template − 5 fired = 4 declined; 10 Grain − 6 fired = 4 declined;
6 Marks − 5 fired = 1 declined; 6 Presence − 2 fired = 4 declined — total
5+4+4+1+4 = 18), against 22 that fired, for 40 tells total.

## Question 3 — does the rewrite state any fact, name, number, date, quotation or citation not in the source

No. I checked the rewrite against the source line by line before delivering
it. Two places were close enough to call out rather than smooth over:

- The rewrite links the "Prediction accuracy" bullet to "the new engine."
  The source never makes that link inside the bullet itself, but it does
  make it two paragraphs later, verbatim: "the new engine could potentially
  reduce planning errors." I treated that as license to state a connection
  the source draws explicitly elsewhere in the same document, not as an
  invented fact.
- I deliberately did not write "iOS 15 no longer receives security updates
  **from Apple**." Apple is the obvious real-world referent, but the source
  never names Apple, so the rewrite doesn't either — that omission was a
  fabrication I caught and removed rather than one I missed.

Everything else in the rewrite — the version number, the three features, the
audience, the engine rebuild, the error-reduction and adoption forecasts, the
spring-tide mechanism, the "slack drift" term, and the iOS 15 decision — is
restated or trimmed from the source, never added to it.
